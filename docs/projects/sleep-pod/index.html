Sleepal Pod at CES

A full night’s experience in a three-minute demo.

0. Project overview

Goal

Eight hours of sleep.
Three minutes to experience it.

Designed a self-guided demo for a contactless sleep monitor. CES visitors could lie down, turn over and get up to understand relaxation, monitoring and wake-up features.

My role

Project lead
UX & UI design

Experience design: selected moments from a full night that best conveyed the product’s value.
Hardware–software interaction: connected screen actions, device feedback and the experience flow.
CES delivery: coordinated the team to turn the design into a working demonstration.

Outcome

Built trust at CES.
Reused in marketing and the app.

On-site validation: delivered the CES demo and validated the core journey, building confidence among visitors.
Marketing reuse: the experience became crowdfunding campaign content.
Product continuity: adapted the experience for the mobile app.

1. Experience flow

From heart rate and breathing to relaxation and wake-up. On-site footage brings each step to life.

Sense your heartbeat and breathing. No wearable needed.

First ask visitors to stay still, then explain that heart rate and breathing are being detected, making contactless sensing understandable.

Start from this stage

2. Design decisions

Turn overnight monitoring into a short, tangible experience that works on site.

Detection window · Key trade-off

Show progress while waiting.
Reveal readings step by step.

Visitors had about three minutes, but heart rate and breathing measurements needed time. I proposed shorter detection windows or a staged display of metrics, working with the algorithm team to adjust the sequence.

Monitoring conditionsContinuous overnight sensing

product in stages
  1. Start sensing
  2. Show progress

Adjust detection windows and display timing

Environment

Recreate bedroom sensing conditions at a trade show

Nearby signals could interfere with monitoring. Used RF-absorbing foam to shield unwanted signals, approximate a bedroom environment and improve demo stability.

Signal noise→RF shielding→Controlled space

Self-guided flow

Make the next step clear

Condensed a full night into key actions: lying down, turning over and getting up. Screen prompts and device feedback guided visitors through the experience.

Prompt→Action→Feedback

System integration

Connect movement, device and screen

Worked with algorithm, embedded, software and hardware teams to connect sensing results, device responses and interface states, validating the complete interaction loop.

Sensing→Device response→UI update

On-site delivery

Make the design work in the real venue

Resolved on-site network and device connectivity issues, completed booth setup and validated the end-to-end flow to deliver a working demonstration.

Connectivity→Booth setup→Flow validation
